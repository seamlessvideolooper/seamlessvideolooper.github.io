Domesticated on your hardware

Time stops here.

Seamless Video Looper finds where a captured clip can loop: it searches for the frame where an ending can pass for a beginning, then renders that loop to play forward for as long as you want to watch it. The search and the render both happen on your phone; nothing is uploaded.

Why it exists

Repeat is playback. A seamless loop is editing.

A note from the SciNTech team

We finally defeated entropy. Not in a lab, not at large — just here, in the length of one captured moment, we built an eternal engine and found a way to stop time. It runs as an artificial intelligence domesticated on your hardware: it doesn't travel through a network cable to do its work, doesn't sit anywhere but the phone in your hand. Point it at a piece of footage and it goes looking, quietly, for the seam — the exact frame where an ending could pass, unnoticed, for a beginning.

It doesn't invent that frame, it finds it: a place the motion was already circling back to. Once it has one, it renders a loop that keeps moving forward, the same direction the moment always moved in, arriving nowhere and ending never. Watch it once and it feels like a video. Watch it a while and it stops feeling like a video at all — more like a room you can walk back into, the same water still moving, the same light still falling, ready whenever you are.

That's the whole trick: pause any captured moment, relive it, and don't stop reliving it. A plane of eternity, sized to fit in your pocket.

— The SciNTech team

Made for movement

Where looping comes alive.

The strongest source clips contain motion that can continue naturally: water, smoke, clouds, foliage, ambient scenes, gentle camera drift, and generated video with a wandering endpoint.

ORGANIC

GENERATED

Repair endpoint drift

Search a generated clip for a better handoff when its first and last frames do not quite meet.

Choose the right footage

Not every motion can close cleanly.

Fast foreground crossings, occlusion, abrupt lighting changes, and large viewpoint shifts can leave ghosting or warped detail. Another candidate, a shorter blend, or a simpler source section often works better.

Private by design

Your videos are processed on your device.

Video analysis and rendering stay local. The app uses network access for feedback, crash and hardware reports, and a small technical record when you share a result. Video files are not uploaded through those services.
